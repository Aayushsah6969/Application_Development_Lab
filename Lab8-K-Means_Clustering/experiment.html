3. Feature Selection & Scaling¶

For clustering based on purchasing patterns we use:

  • Annual Income (k$) – proxy for customer wealth
  • Spending Score (1-100) – proxy for purchasing behaviour

Both features are scaled to zero mean and unit variance so that neither dominates the distance metric.

4. Choosing the Optimal Number of Clusters¶

4.1 Elbow Method (WCSS)¶

In [7]:
wcss = []
k_range = range(1, 11)

for k in k_range:
    kmeans = KMeans(n_clusters=k, init='k-means++', n_init=10, random_state=42)
    kmeans.fit(X_scaled)
    wcss.append(kmeans.inertia_)

sil_scores = []
k_range_sil = range(2, 11)

for k in k_range_sil:

def segment_label(income, spend):
    il = 'High-Income'   if income >= 60 else 'Medium-Income' if income >= 40 else 'Low-Income'
    sl = 'High-Spending' if spend  >= 60 else 'Moderate-Spending' if spend >= 40 else 'Low-Spending'
    return f'{il} / {sl}'

9. Results & Interpretation¶

Running K-Means with k = 5 (confirmed by the Elbow method and Silhouette score) produces five well-separated customer segments:

Cluster Avg Income (k$) Avg Spending Score Segment Description
0 ~55 ~50 Moderate income, moderate spending – average customers
1 ~26 ~79 Low income, high spending – impulsive / lifestyle spenders
2 ~86 ~82 High income, high spending – prime / premium customers
3 ~88 ~17 High income, low spending – conservative savers
4 ~26 ~21 Low income, low spending – budget-conscious customers

Exact cluster IDs may vary between runs — use the printed centroid table to confirm assignments.

Key Takeaways¶

  • High-income, high-spending (Cluster 2): most valuable segment — target with loyalty programmes and premium product launches.
  • Low-income, low-spending (Cluster 4): price-sensitive — respond best to discount campaigns and value-for-money promotions.
  • High-income, low-spending (Cluster 3): untapped opportunity — personalised recommendations and exclusive offers could convert them.
  • Low-income, high-spending (Cluster 1): impulse buyers — EMI schemes, flash sales, and BNPL options suit this group.
  • Moderate segment (Cluster 0): largest group — general marketing campaigns are most cost-effective here.
